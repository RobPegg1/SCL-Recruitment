The supply chain talent crunch: what UK operators can actually do about it

By Rob Pegg · July 2026

Hiring · Market

If you run a logistics or supply chain operation in the UK, you already know hiring is hard. Vacancies stay open longer, shortlists are thinner, and the people you most want to keep are the ones getting called by other businesses. That is not a temporary blip. It is a structural feature of the sector, and it rewards operators who treat hiring as a discipline rather than an emergency.

Why the market is tight

Three forces are working against you at once. The workforce is ageing, and experienced drivers, shift managers and planners are retiring faster than younger people are entering the sector. The work itself has an image problem with candidates who have never seen a well-run operation. And the businesses competing with you for the same people now include e-commerce operators, parcel networks and manufacturers who have all discovered they need the same skills you do.

What doesn't work

Posting the same advert on the same job board at the same salary and hoping this quarter is different. Neither does inflating a job title to disguise a thin package, or running a five-stage interview process for a role the candidate can get elsewhere in two conversations. In a tight market, the process itself is part of the offer.

What actually works

First, price the role honestly before you go to market. Find out what the market is paying, not what you paid three years ago. If the budget will not reach, change the role, not the adverts.

Second, move at the market's speed, not your governance's. The best operational candidates are in and out of the market in weeks. A decision cycle that takes a month will systematically deliver you the people nobody else wanted.

Third, sell the operation, not the brand. Serious operators choose jobs on the state of the operation, the quality of the kit, the calibre of the leadership and the honesty of the numbers. Show them those things in the process and you separate yourself from every business that only shows them a slide deck.

Fourth, look after the leaders you have. The cheapest hire is the one you never have to make, and retention in this sector is mostly a leadership problem wearing a pay-packet disguise.

The honest conclusion

The talent crunch is not going away, and no recruiter, including this one, can conjure candidates who do not exist. What a good partner can do is tell you the truth about the market before you spend three months learning it the hard way, and reach the people who are not answering adverts. That is the difference between recruiting and hoping.
