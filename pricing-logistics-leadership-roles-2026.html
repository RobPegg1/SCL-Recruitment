How to price a logistics leadership role in 2026

By Rob Pegg · July 2026

Pay · Market Data

Every failed search I see starts the same way: a role priced on what the business paid last time, not what the market is paying now. Here is the verified data you should anchor to before taking any logistics leadership role to market in 2026, and how to use it.

The anchors, from primary sources

Median gross annual earnings for UK full-time employees reached £39,039 in April 2025, up 4.3% on the year, according to the Office for National Statistics Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings published in October 2025. Managers, directors and senior officials saw among the strongest weekly earnings growth of any occupational group at 6.7%. At the very top of the scale, the median for chief executives and senior officials stood at £99,944.

At the floor of your cost base, the National Living Wage rose to £12.71 an hour on 1 April 2026, a 4.1% increase confirmed by the Government at the Autumn Budget. The voluntary Real Living Wage now sits at £13.45. Every increase at the bottom compresses your pay structure from below: if your warehouse floor moved 4.1% and your shift managers moved 2%, the differential that makes supervision worth taking just shrank, and your best team leaders noticed.

What the averages don't tell you

National medians are anchors, not answers. Logistics leadership pay in practice is set by five things the averages cannot see: the size of the P&L, the shift pattern the role actually carries, the scarcity of the licence or compliance requirements attached to it, the state of the operation the leader inherits, and the region. Two general manager roles with the same title can sit £25,000 apart for entirely rational reasons.

How to price a role properly

Start from the verified anchors above, then adjust for the five factors honestly. Benchmark against live vacancies for genuinely comparable operations, not job titles. Decide your position: paying at median gets you median candidates in a market where the good ones have options. And price the whole package, because operators compare bonus structure, car allowance and pension in their heads faster than most HR teams do on paper.

Above all, decide the number before the search starts, not at offer stage. The most expensive discovery in recruitment is finding your budget's ceiling through a declined offer, four weeks after your second-choice candidate accepted elsewhere.

Sources

ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings 2025 (published 23 October 2025); GOV.UK, National Living Wage increase to £12.71 (effective 1 April 2026); Living Wage Foundation, Real Living Wage rates 2025-26. Figures correct at time of writing.
